MOJAD GOOS — TRUST CONSTITUTION

1. Constitutional Purpose

This Constitution establishes the highest-level governance commitments for Trust within MOJAD GOOS.

It defines the rights, responsibilities, boundaries, safeguards, and authority that govern how Trust may be created, evaluated, used, protected, challenged, corrected, and evolved.

This Constitution exists to ensure that Trust remains:

- Human-centered
- Privacy-preserving
- Security-conscious
- Explainable
- Contextual
- Correctable
- Auditable
- Fair
- Accountable
- Resistant to manipulation
- Compatible with long-term technological evolution

Trust is infrastructure.

Trust is not merely a score.

Trust must never become unrestricted power over people.

---

2. Constitutional Authority

This Constitution operates under the MOJAD GOOS "ARCHITECTURE_LAWS.md".

All Trust-related architecture, engineering, policies, models, rules, APIs, AI systems, economic mechanisms, and integrations must remain consistent with this Constitution.

Lower-level implementation decisions must not silently override constitutional protections.

Where a lower-level design conflicts with this Constitution, the conflict must be reviewed and resolved before dependent implementation proceeds.

---

3. Human Dignity and Protection

MOJAD GOOS exists to serve people.

No Trust mechanism may intentionally:

- Dehumanize users
- Manipulate users
- Unreasonably discriminate against users
- Expose unnecessary personal information
- Create irreversible punishment from an incorrect system decision
- Convert Trust into unrestricted control
- Remove legitimate opportunities without appropriate safeguards

System optimization must never take priority over fundamental human protection.

---

4. Identity Is Not Trust

Identity establishes an entity.

Identity does not automatically establish Trust.

The constitutional relationship is:

Identity
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Evaluation
   ↓
Trust State

A verified identity must not automatically receive unlimited Trust.

Trust must be based on appropriate evidence, behavior, context, verification, contribution, and applicable governance rules.

---

5. Privacy Is a Constitutional Right

Privacy must be protected by design.

MOJAD GOOS must not expose personal information merely because the system technically possesses it.

The default principle is:

«Collect the minimum necessary data, protect it appropriately, and disclose the minimum necessary information.»

Private information must not become public Trust evidence merely because it exists.

---

6. Private Evidence Protection

MOJAD GOOS must distinguish between underlying evidence and public Trust indicators.

The constitutional pattern is:

Identity
   ↓
Private Evidence
   ↓
Trust Processing
   ↓
Public Indicator
   ↓
Opportunity Signal

Public indicators should communicate appropriate Trust information without unnecessarily revealing the private evidence behind them.

The existence of a Trust indicator must not imply unrestricted access to the evidence supporting it.

---

7. Data Minimization

MOJAD GOOS must minimize:

- Data collection
- Data exposure
- Data retention
- Data duplication
- Data transmission
- Data access

More data must not be collected merely because it may become useful in the future.

Any sensitive data capability must have a legitimate purpose and appropriate controls.

---

8. Contextual Trust

Trust must be contextual.

Trust in one domain must not automatically become universal Trust.

For example:

Professional Trust
      ≠
Financial Trust
      ≠
Marketplace Trust
      ≠
Community Trust
      ≠
Government-Service Trust

Trust indicators must preserve the context in which the evidence was generated.

---

9. Trust Must Be Earned

Trust must be earned through appropriate evidence and responsible behavior.

Trust must not simply be:

- Purchased
- Assigned arbitrarily
- Manufactured through manipulation
- Granted permanently without review

Economic participation may create legitimate evidence in some contexts, but financial capacity alone must never automatically equal universal Trust.

---

10. Trust Must Be Explainable

Important Trust decisions must have an understandable basis.

Where appropriate, an affected user should be able to understand:

- What happened
- What type of Trust decision occurred
- What factors were relevant
- What rules applied
- What can be challenged
- What correction mechanisms exist

Explanation must protect sensitive security and privacy information where disclosure would create additional risk.

---

11. Trust Must Be Auditable

Critical Trust operations must be appropriately auditable.

This includes, where applicable:

- Verification changes
- Trust-state changes
- Permission changes
- Administrative actions
- Important configuration changes
- Appeals
- Corrections
- Security events
- Model changes

Auditability must not become unrestricted surveillance.

Audit data must itself be protected.

---

12. Trust Must Be Correctable

MOJAD GOOS must provide appropriate mechanisms for correcting inaccurate or outdated Trust information.

Users should have appropriate pathways for:

- Correction
- Re-verification
- Appeal
- Review
- Dispute
- Recovery from system error

No user should be permanently defined by an uncorrectable error.

---

13. Right to Appropriate Due Process

Where Trust information materially affects access to important opportunities or services, MOJAD GOOS should provide appropriate procedural safeguards.

Depending on the context, safeguards may include:

- Notice
- Explanation
- Review
- Appeal
- Correction
- Re-verification
- Human oversight

The exact mechanism may differ by domain and risk level.

---

14. Trust Is Not Absolute Power

Trust must never automatically grant unrestricted:

- Data access
- Administrative authority
- Financial authority
- Identity access
- AI authority
- Governance authority
- Platform control

Trust and permission are separate concepts.

Trust
  ≠
Permission
  ≠
Authority

Each must be governed independently.

---

15. Least Privilege

Access to Trust information and systems must follow least privilege.

Every actor must receive only the access necessary for its legitimate purpose.

This applies to:

- Users
- Administrators
- Developers
- Services
- AI systems
- APIs
- Partners
- External integrations

Access must be appropriately scoped, auditable, and revocable.

---

16. Security as a Trust Requirement

Trust cannot be considered reliable if its underlying systems are insecure.

Trust infrastructure must therefore incorporate appropriate:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Encryption
- Isolation
- Threat modeling
- Monitoring
- Abuse prevention
- Incident response
- Recovery

Security must be designed into Trust infrastructure rather than added after implementation.

---

17. Anti-Manipulation Principle

MOJAD GOOS must actively consider attempts to manipulate Trust.

Potential manipulation includes:

- Fake activity
- Sybil behavior
- Collusion
- Fraudulent verification
- Reputation farming
- Referral abuse
- Artificial engagement
- Coordinated manipulation
- Economic exploitation

Trust mechanisms must not reward behavior merely because it increases a metric.

---

18. No Single Trust Metric

MOJAD GOOS should not depend on one universal Trust number as the sole representation of a person or organization.

Trust may require multiple contextual indicators.

A single number can hide:

- Context
- Uncertainty
- Time
- Evidence quality
- Domain differences

Where aggregated indicators are used, their meaning and limitations must remain clear.

---

19. Trust and Opportunity

Trust exists partly to help people interact with greater confidence and access legitimate opportunities.

The constitutional relationship is:

Identity
   ↓
Trust
   ↓
Opportunity
   ↓
Action
   ↓
Prosperity
   ↓
Human Progress

However, Trust must not become an arbitrary permanent gatekeeper.

Opportunity systems must preserve appropriate fairness, context, correction, and transparency.

---

20. Fairness and Non-Arbitrary Treatment

Trust systems must avoid arbitrary treatment based on protected or irrelevant characteristics.

Trust decisions should rely on legitimate evidence relevant to the specific context.

Where automated systems may produce unequal outcomes, appropriate evaluation and safeguards should be considered.

---

21. AI Constitutional Boundary

AI systems operating within MOJAD GOOS must remain subordinate to constitutional Trust, Privacy, Security, and Governance requirements.

AI must not automatically receive unrestricted access to:

- Identity data
- Private evidence
- Trust systems
- Administrative controls
- Economic systems
- Production infrastructure

AI access must be appropriately:

- Scoped
- Authorized
- Audited
- Monitored
- Revocable

AI output must not automatically become constitutional truth.

---

22. Human Accountability

Automation does not eliminate responsibility.

Where Trust decisions have significant consequences, MOJAD GOOS must define appropriate accountability mechanisms.

Human oversight should be available where the risk, impact, or uncertainty justifies it.

---

23. Economic Neutrality of Trust

Trust must not become a mechanism through which wealth automatically determines human worth.

Economic activity may provide legitimate evidence in economic contexts.

However:

Money
  ≠
Identity
  ≠
Trust
  ≠
Human Worth

Economic mechanisms must remain subject to Trust, Security, Privacy, and anti-manipulation safeguards.

---

24. External Platform and Partner Boundaries

External organizations, platforms, applications, and partners must not receive unrestricted Trust merely because they participate in the MOJAD ecosystem.

Every integration must have explicit:

- Identity
- Purpose
- Scope
- Permissions
- Data boundaries
- Security requirements
- Revocation mechanisms

Trust in an organization does not imply unrestricted access to user data.

---

25. Pi Integration Boundary

Where MOJAD GOOS integrates with Pi Network or Pi ecosystem services, all integration must respect:

- Officially available capabilities
- Applicable permissions
- User consent where required
- Data minimization
- Security requirements
- Technical limitations
- Official ecosystem rules

MOJAD GOOS must not represent an unverified Pi capability as an available production capability.

Pi integration must never be used as justification for bypassing MOJAD privacy or security requirements.

---

26. Data Separation

Sensitive Trust data should be logically separated according to sensitivity and purpose.

At minimum, architecture should distinguish appropriately between:

Public Information
      ↓
Limited Information
      ↓
Private Information
      ↓
Highly Sensitive Evidence
      ↓
Security-Critical Information

The exact classifications may evolve through the Privacy and Security architectures.

---

27. User Control

Users should have appropriate visibility and control over their own information.

Depending on the data and legal/security context, users may require mechanisms to:

- Review information
- Correct information
- Manage consent
- Revoke permissions
- Appeal decisions
- Understand important Trust indicators

User control must coexist with legitimate security, legal, fraud-prevention, and governance requirements.

---

28. Trust Lifecycle

Trust must be treated as a lifecycle rather than a permanent label.

Created
   ↓
Verified
   ↓
Observed
   ↓
Evaluated
   ↓
Updated
   ↓
Reviewed
   ↓
Corrected / Maintained / Reduced
   ↓
Retired where appropriate

Trust states must be capable of changing when evidence, context, or circumstances change.

---

29. Temporal Integrity

Trust evidence may become outdated.

MOJAD GOOS must consider:

- Evidence age
- Relevance
- Recency
- Expiration
- Re-verification

Old evidence must not automatically be treated as current evidence forever.

---

30. Uncertainty

Trust systems must acknowledge uncertainty.

Absence of evidence must not automatically be interpreted as evidence of wrongdoing.

Likewise, limited evidence must not automatically be interpreted as high Trust.

Where appropriate, systems should distinguish between:

- Verified
- Supported
- Unknown
- Uncertain
- Disputed
- Expired
- Invalid

---

31. No Irreversible Trust Decisions by Default

Trust-related consequences should be reversible where reasonably possible.

High-impact and irreversible actions require stronger review and justification.

Architecture should prefer:

Detect
 ↓
Restrict where necessary
 ↓
Review
 ↓
Correct / Confirm
 ↓
Restore or Apply Decision

rather than immediate irreversible punishment.

---

32. Separation of Duties

Critical Trust operations should not depend unnecessarily on one uncontrolled actor.

Where appropriate, critical functions should separate:

- Verification
- Evaluation
- Administration
- Security
- Appeals
- Auditing

This reduces the risk of abuse and conflicts of interest.

---

33. Constitutional Protection Against Surveillance

Trust infrastructure must not become a justification for unlimited monitoring of human behavior.

Monitoring must have:

- Legitimate purpose
- Appropriate scope
- Appropriate retention
- Access controls
- Security protections
- Accountability

The goal is trustworthy infrastructure, not permanent surveillance.

---

34. Constitutional Protection Against Manipulation

MOJAD GOOS must not intentionally design Trust mechanisms to psychologically manipulate users into:

- Unnecessary spending
- Unnecessary engagement
- Unnecessary disclosure
- Unhealthy dependency
- Artificial activity

User value must remain the primary objective.

---

35. Transparency of System Capability

MOJAD GOOS must clearly distinguish between:

- Production capabilities
- Planned capabilities
- Experimental capabilities
- Simulated capabilities
- Unavailable capabilities

Trust in the system depends on truthful representation of what the system actually does.

---

36. Architectural Independence

Trust constitutional requirements must remain valid even if the underlying technology changes.

Technology may evolve from:

- Current web infrastructure
- Blockchain systems
- AI systems
- New identity technologies
- New cryptographic technologies
- Future distributed systems

The constitutional protections must remain stable unless formally revised.

---

37. Constitutional Amendment

This Constitution may evolve when legitimate technological, legal, social, security, or ecosystem requirements change.

However, constitutional changes must be:

- Explicit
- Documented
- Reviewed
- Versioned
- Justified
- Backward-impact assessed where appropriate

Changes must not silently weaken fundamental protections.

---

38. Conflict Resolution

When two Trust requirements appear to conflict, the system must prioritize:

1. Human safety and dignity
2. Privacy
3. Security
4. Constitutional protections
5. Legal and governance requirements
6. Correctness and accountability
7. Legitimate user value
8. Operational efficiency

This ordering may require contextual interpretation in specific domains.

---

39. Constitutional Review Gate

Before major Trust infrastructure is implemented, the architecture should be reviewed against this Constitution.

Review should ask:

- Does the design protect human dignity?
- Does it preserve privacy?
- Does it preserve security?
- Is Identity separated from Trust?
- Is Trust contextual?
- Is evidence protected?
- Is access least-privilege?
- Is the system auditable?
- Is it correctable?
- Is it resistant to manipulation?
- Is AI appropriately bounded?
- Are economic incentives safe?
- Are external integrations controlled?
- Is the design future-compatible?

A critical failure must block dependent implementation until resolved.
