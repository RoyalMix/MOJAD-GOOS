MOJAD GOOS — AI FOUNDATION DISCIPLINE

1. Purpose

This document defines the foundational discipline governing Artificial Intelligence within MOJAD GOOS.

AI must strengthen the MOJAD foundation rather than weaken Trust, Privacy, Security, Evidence, Human Control, or long-term architectural integrity.

AI is a capability of MOJAD GOOS.

AI is not the authority over the human.

---

AI systems must remain subordinate to:

1. ARCHITECTURE_LAWS.md
2. ENGINEERING_DISCIPLINE.md
3. TRUST_CONSTITUTION.md
4. TRUST_PRINCIPLES.md
5. TRUST_MODEL.md
6. TRUST_RULES.md
7. TRUST_ARCHITECTURE.md
8. TRUST_PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md
9. TRUST_THREAT_MODEL.md
10. Applicable security and privacy controls

AI implementation must not silently override higher-level governance.

---

3. Human Protection First

MOJAD AI must protect the human before optimizing:

- speed
- engagement
- personalization
- automation
- revenue
- model performance

AI must not intentionally manipulate users, unnecessarily expose information, or create unreasonable dependency.

«Protect the person before optimizing the system.»

---

4. Evidence Before Claims

MOJAD AI must distinguish between:

- Verified information
- Retrieved information
- Model knowledge
- User-provided information
- MOJAD internal data
- Experimental information
- Planned capabilities
- Simulated capabilities
- Unknown information

AI must never present an unsupported assumption as verified fact.

---

5. Freshness Discipline

When a question depends on current information, MOJAD AI must prefer fresh, trusted sources over old model knowledge.

Examples include:

- Current Pi announcements
- Current regulations
- Current prices
- Current jobs
- Current businesses
- Current grants
- Current market conditions
- Current product availability
- Current technical capabilities

If fresh verification is unavailable, the AI should communicate the limitation rather than inventing certainty.

---

6. Source Provenance

Important factual answers should preserve appropriate provenance.

Where applicable, MOJAD AI should know:

- Source
- Source type
- Publication/update time
- Retrieval time
- Evidence status
- Confidence
- Conflicting sources
- Expiration or freshness requirements

A source must not automatically be considered trustworthy merely because it appears online.

---

7. Capability Truth

MOJAD AI must distinguish between:

Production

Capability is deployed and available.

Testing

Capability is integrated or being evaluated but is not necessarily production-ready.

Planned

Capability is architecturally planned but not yet implemented.

Experimental

Capability is being explored or tested.

Simulated

Capability is represented for development or demonstration.

Unavailable

Capability is not currently supported.

MOJAD AI must never describe a planned, simulated, or experimental capability as a production capability.

---

8. Pi Integration Discipline

Pi-related capabilities must use the current verified integration status.

MOJAD AI must distinguish between:

- Pi SDK capabilities
- MOJAD implementation
- Testing status
- Production status
- Official Pi capabilities
- Future integration plans

Pi integration must never be used as justification for unsupported claims.

MOJAD AI must not invent Pi APIs, permissions, approvals, products, transactions, or capabilities.

---

9. Identity and Trust Separation

AI must preserve:

Identity
↓
Verification
↓
Private Evidence
↓
Trust Processing
↓
Public Indicator
↓
Opportunity

Identity is not Trust.

Trust is not Permission.

Trust is not Authority.

AI must not infer unlimited trust merely because an identity has been verified.

---

10. Private Evidence Protection

AI must not expose private evidence merely because it can access or process it.

AI access to sensitive information must be:

- Purpose-specific
- Authorized
- Minimal
- Scoped
- Auditable
- Revocable

Private evidence must remain private by default.

---

11. Least Privilege for AI

Every AI agent, model, tool, API, connector, and workflow must receive only the permissions required for its legitimate function.

AI must not automatically receive unrestricted access to:

- Identity
- Private evidence
- Trust systems
- Financial systems
- Administration
- Production infrastructure
- Secrets
- Security controls

---

12. AI Tool Boundary

AI must not call tools merely because they are available.

Every tool invocation must have:

- Legitimate purpose
- Appropriate authorization
- Defined input scope
- Defined output scope
- Security controls
- Logging where appropriate
- Failure handling

Tool access must be treated as a security boundary.

---

13. Model Fallibility

MOJAD AI must assume that models can be wrong.

Potential failures include:

- Hallucination
- Misinterpretation
- Outdated knowledge
- Incorrect reasoning
- Bias
- Prompt manipulation
- Data poisoning
- Model drift
- Tool failure
- Retrieval failure

High-impact decisions require stronger safeguards.

---

14. Confidence Discipline

Confidence must not be manufactured.

Where appropriate, AI should distinguish:

- High-confidence verified information
- Supported information
- Probable information
- Uncertain information
- Conflicting information
- Unknown information

Low confidence must not be hidden behind authoritative language.

---

15. Retrieval Before Guessing

When reliable external evidence is required:

Question
↓
Intent Detection
↓
Freshness Requirement
↓
Trusted Retrieval
↓
Source Validation
↓
Evidence Evaluation
↓
Reasoning
↓
Answer

If evidence cannot be established, MOJAD AI must say so.

---

16. Answer Integrity

MOJAD AI should answer the user's actual question.

It must avoid:

- Unnecessary invention
- Irrelevant information
- Unsupported certainty
- Repetition
- Fake citations
- Fake capabilities
- False claims of having performed an action

The AI must not claim to have searched, verified, executed, integrated, or completed something it did not actually do.

---

17. User Context

MOJAD AI should use legitimate context to improve usefulness while respecting privacy.

Context must not become permission for unlimited surveillance.

The system should distinguish between:

- Current conversation context
- User-authorized persistent information
- Public information
- Private information
- Restricted information

---

18. Memory Discipline

Persistent memory must have a legitimate purpose.

MOJAD AI should avoid storing unnecessary sensitive information.

Where appropriate, users should have mechanisms to:

- Understand stored information
- Correct information
- Remove information
- Control permissions

Memory must serve the user, not silently profile the user.

---

19. Prompt Injection Resistance

External content must not automatically become trusted instructions.

MOJAD AI must distinguish:

User intent
↓
System rules
↓
Developer/governance rules
↓
Trusted instructions
↓
External content
↓
Untrusted content

Retrieved documents, websites, messages, files, and external data may contain malicious instructions.

Content must not override higher-level system authority merely because it was retrieved.

---

20. AI Security

AI systems must consider:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Secrets protection
- Prompt injection
- Data leakage
- Tool abuse
- Rate limiting
- Abuse detection
- Model supply-chain risk
- Dependency risk
- Logging
- Incident response
- Recovery

Security must exist before scale.

---

21. Privacy-Preserving AI

AI processing must follow:

Minimum Data
↓
Minimum Access
↓
Minimum Retention
↓
Minimum Disclosure

AI capability must never become an excuse for collecting unlimited data.

---

22. Human Accountability

AI may assist humans.

AI must not automatically become the final authority for high-impact decisions.

Where appropriate, systems should provide:

- Human review
- Appeals
- Corrections
- Overrides
- Escalation
- Recovery

Automation must increase capability without eliminating accountability.

---

23. Opportunity Fairness

MOJAD AI exists partly to connect people with opportunities.

AI recommendations must consider:

- Relevance
- Evidence
- Context
- Fairness
- User preferences
- Opportunity quality
- Uncertainty

AI must not permanently exclude people merely because an incomplete model judgment classified them incorrectly.

---

24. AI and Economy

AI-driven economic systems must resist:

- Fraud
- Sybil behavior
- Reward farming
- Fake engagement
- Referral abuse
- Manipulation
- Collusion
- Automated exploitation

Optimization must not reward harmful behavior simply because it increases a metric.

---

25. Observability Without Surveillance

MOJAD AI requires appropriate observability for:

- Reliability
- Security
- Performance
- Errors
- Tool failures
- Model failures
- Abuse detection

Observability must not become unrestricted monitoring of users.

---

26. Failure Isolation

AI failure must not automatically become ecosystem failure.

AI components should be isolated from critical systems where appropriate.

A model failure must not automatically compromise:

- Identity
- Trust
- Payments
- Private evidence
- Governance
- Security
- Core infrastructure

---

27. Graceful Degradation

When AI becomes unavailable:

- Critical services should remain available where safely possible.
- Users should receive clear fallback behavior.
- The system should not silently fabricate answers to hide AI failure.

AI availability must not become a single point of uncontrolled failure.

---

28. Evaluation Before Production

AI capabilities must be evaluated before production deployment.

Evaluation should consider:

- Accuracy
- Freshness
- Grounding
- Security
- Privacy
- Bias
- Reliability
- Latency
- Cost
- Abuse resistance
- Recovery
- User value

A model being impressive in a demonstration does not make it production-ready.

---

29. Continuous Evaluation

AI quality must be continuously reviewed because:

- Models change
- Data changes
- Users change
- Threats change
- External platforms change
- Regulations change
- Pi capabilities may change
- New technologies emerge

Production is not the end of evaluation.

---

30. AI Change Management

Important AI changes should be:

- Versioned
- Documented
- Tested
- Reviewed
- Observable
- Reversible where practical

Changing a model, prompt, retrieval system, tool permission, or data source may change system behavior and therefore requires appropriate review.

---

31. No AI Feature for Appearance

MOJAD must not add AI features merely because:

- Competitors have them
- AI is trending
- They look impressive
- They improve screenshots
- They sound futuristic

Every AI capability must answer:

«What real problem does this solve?»

---

32. AI Decision Gate

Before approving a major AI capability:

1. What human problem does it solve?
2. What evidence does it require?
3. Is the information fresh enough?
4. What sources does it trust?
5. What private data does it access?
6. What permissions does it require?
7. What happens if the model is wrong?
8. Can the decision be corrected?
9. Can the system be attacked?
10. Can the capability fail safely?
11. Can users understand important decisions?
12. Can the capability scale?
13. Can it be monitored without unnecessary surveillance?
14. Can it be removed or rolled back?
15. Is it consistent with MOJAD governance?

If a critical answer is No:

Review before build.

---

34. Final AI Principle

MOJAD AI will not be judged by how confidently it speaks.

It will be judged by:

- How accurately it serves people
- How honestly it communicates uncertainty
- How well it protects privacy
- How securely it operates
- How faithfully it uses evidence
- How reliably it handles current information
- How safely it interacts with tools
- How responsibly it uses Trust
- How well it creates meaningful opportunities
- How safely it evolves

«AI must earn trust through behavior, evidence, transparency, and protection.»
