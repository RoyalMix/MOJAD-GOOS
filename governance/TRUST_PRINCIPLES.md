# MOJAD GOOS — TRUST PRINCIPLES

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the fundamental behavioral principles that guide how Trust should be designed, evaluated, represented, protected, and evolved within MOJAD GOOS.

The TRUST_CONSTITUTION defines the constitutional protections and boundaries.

This document defines the principles that translate those constitutional commitments into a coherent Trust philosophy.

These principles guide:

- Trust architecture
- Trust models
- Trust rules
- Verification
- Evidence evaluation
- Reputation
- Opportunity systems
- AI-assisted Trust
- Security
- Privacy
- Economic mechanisms
- Governance
- APIs and integrations
- Future Trust technologies

Trust is infrastructure.

Trust is not merely a number.

# 3. Core Trust Principles

## Principle 1 — Trust Must Be Earned

Trust should emerge from appropriate evidence, verified actions, responsible behavior, meaningful contribution, and consistent participation.

Trust should not simply be assigned because an entity requests it.

Trust must have a defensible basis.

---

## Principle 2 — Identity Is Not Trust

Identity establishes who or what an entity is.

Identity does not automatically establish Trust.

The fundamental relationship is:

Identity
↓
Verification
↓
Evidence
↓
Evaluation
↓
Trust State

A verified identity may still have limited evidence in a particular context.

---

## Principle 3 — Trust Is Contextual

Trust must be evaluated according to the activity, domain, relationship, and risk involved.

Professional Trust ≠ Financial Trust ≠ Marketplace Trust ≠ Community Trust ≠ Governance Trust.

Trust should not automatically transfer from one context to another.

---

## Principle 4 — Private Evidence Must Remain Protected

Underlying Trust evidence should remain private by default unless legitimate authorization requires otherwise.

The preferred architecture is:

Identity
↓
Private Evidence
↓
Trust Processing
↓
Public Indicator
↓
Opportunity Signal

A public Trust indicator should reveal only what is necessary for the legitimate purpose.

Trust should never become an excuse for unnecessary disclosure.

---

## Principle 5 — Trust Indicators Are Not Raw Evidence

MOJAD GOOS should separate:

Evidence
↓
Evaluation
↓
Trust State
↓
Public Indicator

A user should not need to expose all underlying evidence simply to demonstrate an appropriate Trust state.

Indicators must not become backdoors into private information.

---

## Principle 6 — Trust Must Be Explainable

Important Trust outcomes should have understandable reasoning appropriate to the context.

Where appropriate, users should be able to understand:

- What happened
- What type of Trust decision occurred
- What factors mattered
- What evidence category was relevant
- What rules applied
- Whether uncertainty exists
- How the decision can be challenged

Security-sensitive information may require controlled disclosure.

---

## Principle 7 — Trust Must Be Correctable

Trust is not permanent truth.

Evidence can be:

- Incorrect
- Outdated
- Incomplete
- Misinterpreted
- Disputed
- Compromised

Therefore Trust systems must support:

Detect
↓
Review
↓
Correct
↓
Re-evaluate
↓
Update Trust State

No person should be permanently defined by an uncorrectable system error.

---

## Principle 8 — Trust Must Reflect Evidence Quality

Not all evidence has equal strength.

Trust evaluation should consider appropriate dimensions such as:

- Source reliability
- Verification status
- Recency
- Relevance
- Consistency
- Context
- Independence
- Potential manipulation

More evidence does not automatically mean stronger evidence.

---

## Principle 9 — Trust Must Recognize Uncertainty

MOJAD GOOS must not pretend to know what it cannot reliably establish.

Where appropriate, Trust systems should distinguish between:

- Verified
- Supported
- Unknown
- Uncertain
- Disputed
- Expired
- Invalid

Absence of evidence must not automatically become evidence of wrongdoing.

---

## Principle 10 — Trust Must Evolve Over Time

Trust should be treated as a lifecycle.

Created
↓
Verified
↓
Observed
↓
Evaluated
↓
Updated
↓
Reviewed
↓
Maintained / Reduced / Corrected
↓
Retired where appropriate

Old evidence should not automatically remain equally valuable forever.

---

## Principle 11 — Trust Must Be Proportional to Risk

Higher-risk actions require stronger evidence and stronger safeguards.

Required Trust should depend on:

Context
+
Risk
+
Impact
+
Evidence Quality
+
Authorization

Low-risk activities should not require unnecessarily invasive verification.

High-risk activities should not rely on weak evidence merely for convenience.

---

## Principle 12 — Trust Does Not Equal Permission

Trust, permission, and authority are separate concepts.

Trust ≠ Permission ≠ Authority.

A highly trusted entity may still have restricted access.

Trust must never automatically grant:

- Private data access
- Administrative authority
- Financial authority
- AI authority
- Governance authority
- Infrastructure access

---

## Principle 13 — Least Privilege Applies to Trust

Trust-related access must follow least privilege.

Every actor should receive only the access necessary for its legitimate purpose.

This applies to:

- Users
- Developers
- Administrators
- Services
- AI systems
- APIs
- Partners
- External integrations

Trust should increase confidence, not eliminate security boundaries.

---

## Principle 14 — Trust Must Resist Manipulation

Trust systems must be designed against attempts to manufacture Trust artificially.

Potential manipulation includes:

- Sybil attacks
- Fake activity
- Reputation farming
- Collusion
- Referral abuse
- Fake verification
- Coordinated manipulation
- Economic exploitation
- Artificial engagement

The system should evaluate the quality and independence of evidence, not merely activity volume.

---

## Principle 15 — Trust Must Not Become a Popularity Contest

High activity, high engagement, many followers, or financial capacity should not automatically equal high Trust.

Popularity can be useful evidence in some contexts, but it must not become a universal Trust mechanism.

Popularity ≠ Trust  
Activity ≠ Trust  
Wealth ≠ Trust  
Verification ≠ Universal Trust

---

## Principle 16 — Trust Must Support Opportunity, Not Arbitrary Exclusion

Trust exists partly to enable safer interaction and better opportunity discovery.

The intended direction is:

Identity
↓
Trust
↓
Opportunity
↓
Action
↓
Prosperity
↓
Human Progress

Trust should improve matching and confidence without becoming an arbitrary permanent gatekeeper.

---

## Principle 17 — Trust Should Increase Confidence, Not Control People

Trust infrastructure must not be designed to create unnecessary dependency, surveillance, manipulation, or loss of user autonomy.

The purpose of Trust is:

More Confidence
+
Better Decisions
+
Safer Interaction

Not:

More Control
+
More Surveillance
+
Less Freedom

---

## Principle 18 — AI Must Respect Trust Boundaries

AI may assist with:

- Evidence analysis
- Pattern detection
- Risk analysis
- Matching
- Explanation
- Trust recommendations

However, AI must operate within explicit authorization and privacy boundaries.

AI should not automatically receive unrestricted access to:

- Identity data
- Private evidence
- Trust records
- Administrative systems
- Economic systems
- Production infrastructure

AI recommendations are not automatically Trust truth.

---

## Principle 19 — Human Accountability Remains Necessary

Automation can increase capability but does not eliminate responsibility.

For appropriately high-impact Trust decisions, MOJAD GOOS should provide mechanisms for:

- Human review
- Appeal
- Correction
- Escalation
- Intervention

The level of human oversight should correspond to risk and impact.

---

## Principle 20 — Trust Must Be Auditable

Important Trust operations should generate appropriate audit evidence.

Examples include:

- Verification changes
- Trust-state changes
- Permission changes
- Administrative actions
- Appeals
- Corrections
- Security events
- Model changes
- Important configuration changes

Auditability must remain privacy-preserving.

---

## Principle 21 — Trust Must Be Privacy-Preserving

Trust systems should follow:

Collect Less
↓
Protect Better
↓
Process Carefully
↓
Expose Less
↓
Delete or Retire When Appropriate

The existence of Trust infrastructure does not justify unlimited data collection.

---

## Principle 22 — Trust Must Be Secure by Design

Trust cannot be reliable if its underlying infrastructure is insecure.

Trust systems should incorporate:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Encryption
- Isolation
- Threat modeling
- Abuse prevention
- Monitoring
- Incident response
- Recovery

Security must exist before Trust is exposed at scale.

---

## Principle 23 — Trust Must Be Separable From Technology

Trust principles must survive technological change.

MOJAD GOOS may evolve through:

- Web technologies
- Blockchain
- AI
- Cryptography
- Distributed systems
- New identity technologies
- Future technologies

The technology may change.

The fundamental human protections should remain stable unless formally revised.

---

## Principle 24 — Trust Must Be Interoperable

Trust information should be represented through controlled, explicit interfaces where interoperability is required.

External systems should not automatically inherit internal Trust authority.

Integrations should define:

- Identity
- Purpose
- Scope
- Permissions
- Data boundaries
- Security requirements
- Revocation

---

## Principle 25 — Trust Must Be Transparent About Its Own Limitations

MOJAD GOOS must clearly distinguish between:

- Verified
- Inferred
- Recommended
- Estimated
- Experimental
- Unknown
- Unavailable

The system must never present an inference as verified fact.

Trust in MOJAD depends partly on honest representation of uncertainty.

---

## Principle 26 — No Universal Trust Score by Default

MOJAD GOOS should avoid treating every person or organization as a single universal number.

Where indicators are necessary, they should preserve:

- Context
- Evidence quality
- Time
- Uncertainty
- Domain
- Risk

A number may summarize information, but it must not replace the underlying Trust model.

---

## Principle 27 — Trust Must Be Proportionate

The system should not require extreme verification for ordinary low-risk interactions.

Likewise, high-impact activities should not rely on weak Trust signals.

Verification and Trust requirements should be proportional to:

Risk
+
Impact
+
Sensitivity
+
Potential Harm

---

## Principle 28 — Trust Must Be Reversible Where Possible

When Trust information changes, the system should prefer controlled state transitions rather than irreversible punishment.

Where appropriate:

Detect
↓
Restrict
↓
Review
↓
Confirm / Correct
↓
Restore / Update

Irreversible decisions require stronger justification and review.

---

## Principle 29 — Trust Must Not Be Purchased

Economic participation may create evidence in legitimate economic contexts.

However:

Money ≠ Trust  
Money ≠ Human Worth  
Money ≠ Universal Authority

Economic incentives must not allow users to buy arbitrary Trust status.

---

## Principle 30 — Trust Must Protect Future Generations

MOJAD GOOS is intended to become infrastructure for generations of:

- Users
- Developers
- Companies
- Communities
- Organizations
- Governments

Trust architecture must remain understandable, maintainable, adaptable, and resistant to short-term manipulation.

# 4. Trust Decision Philosophy

When evaluating a Trust-related decision, MOJAD GOOS should ask:

1. What is the context?
2. What is the legitimate purpose?
3. What evidence is available?
4. How reliable is the evidence?
5. How recent is it?
6. Is the evidence disputed?
7. What uncertainty exists?
8. What risk is involved?
9. What information is actually necessary?
10. What permissions are required?
11. Can the decision be explained?
12. Can it be challenged?
13. Can it be corrected?
14. Can the action be audited?
15. Can the decision be reversed?

No single Trust signal should automatically answer all of these questions.

# 9. Principle Review Gate

Before a Trust principle is implemented technically, review:

### Human Protection

- Does it protect the person?
- Could it create unnecessary control or surveillance?

### Privacy

- Does it minimize data exposure?
- Does private evidence remain protected?

### Security

- Does it respect least privilege?
- Does it preserve security boundaries?

### Trust Quality

- Is the evidence appropriate?
- Is context preserved?
- Is uncertainty represented?

### Fairness

- Could it create arbitrary exclusion?
- Can errors be corrected?

### AI

- Can AI manipulate or overreach?
- Is AI authority appropriately bounded?

### Opportunity

- Does the principle improve legitimate opportunity?
- Could it become an unchallengeable gatekeeper?

### Future

- Will the principle remain valid as technology changes?

A critical failure must block dependent implementation until resolved.
